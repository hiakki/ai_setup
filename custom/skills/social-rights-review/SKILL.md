# Social rights and monetization review

Give the user a concrete publishing route for each requested platform, supported
by current official sources and the actual source asset. This skill is an
evidence workflow, not a legal opinion, rights license or platform certification.

Read [the policy reference](references/platforms.md) for the relevant platform.
Reopen official sources when answering a current-policy question. Record the
check date. Meta Help pages may fail in search/fetch but load in a real browser;
try that read-only fallback before declaring a policy inaccessible. Distinguish
the policy article from any generated support-assistant summary on that page.

## Evidence to distinguish

Assess these independently; do not collapse them into a single safe/eligible score:

- **Source rights:** asset URL, actual owner, origin, license or permission,
  permitted platforms, commercial use and editing conditions, attribution,
  geography/term and audio or other third-party exclusions.
- **Originality:** the specific new argument, explanation, criticism, parody or
  creative contribution in the final video. Inspect the actual edit and script.
- **Content restrictions:** platform-specific formats and advertiser suitability.
- **Account access:** actual program, creator country, account standing,
  thresholds, enrollment/invitation, accepted terms and payout setup.

Mark unsupported items unknown. An AI factual-review pass, no Content ID match,
successful upload, another account's monetization or public source availability
does not establish any missing permission or this account's program approval.
Do not turn current unknown eligibility into a claim of definitive ineligibility.

## Apply to the proposed content

1. Identify all underlying assets, including another creator's recording and
   soundtrack. Self-recorded gameplay and a downloaded gameplay video are
   different rights situations. A publisher policy is not permission from the
   person whose recording was copied.
2. Read the exact permission. Check whether it covers the operator, use,
   platform and revenue mechanism. Native remix permissions are scoped; do not
   assume they permit external downloads or cross-platform reposting.
3. For a copyright exception, describe the relevant jurisdiction and actual
   use. Assess factors rather than declaring a safe duration or edit percentage.
   A license is one route; do not incorrectly state every lawful use needs one.
4. Review originality and monetization independently for YT, FB and IG. Do not
   apply a Facebook newsroom example as an Instagram requirement, or treat
   membership/gifts access as advertising-revenue eligibility.
5. Recommend an actionable route: original recordings under an applicable
   publisher permission, expressly licensed creator clips, original diagrams,
   or another asset with a documented basis. Explain any restrictions on that
   route and distinguish it from the uncleared source under review.

For flips, speed changes, captions or a passive reaction overlay, assess actual
new value; these edits confer no copyright permission. Substantive analysis can
matter, but cannot cure unsupported factual claims. Do not promise that adding
a real face, AI presenter or voice guarantees originality or monetization.

## Deliverable

Lead with the practical conclusion. Provide a per-platform comparison of source
rights, originality and available revenue mechanism, with direct official links.
Separate verified policy, assessment of the actual clip, and account facts not
inspected. Show a viable sourcing example, not only a disclaimer or refusal.
Preserve existing authorization boundaries; this review does not authorize
posting, scheduling, provider spending or changes to publishing gates.
